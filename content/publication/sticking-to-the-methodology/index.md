abstract: Banking supervisors set capital requirements by combining a rule-based methodology with their own judgement. We study whether this discretion is subject to a status quo bias, using the Pillar 2 Requirement (P2R) set for significant institutions in the euro area over 2019–2024. Because the final requirement adjusts an observable methodological starting point, supervisory discretion can be measured directly rather than estimated as a residual. We develop a model in which a supervisor combines the methodology's proposal with soft information on the bank, subject to a private cost of departing from the methodology and a tendency to over-weight soft information. The model predicts an inaction band, asymmetric departures, flatter adjustments where information is noisier, and offsetting revisions across risk areas. Empirically, supervisors are anchored to the methodology, and departures from it are mostly upward, consistent with a higher private cost of setting the requirement below the methodology than above it. Qualitative risk areas follow the methodology more often than quantitative ones, although responses outside the inaction band are not flatter, and risk-area revisions offset each other.
