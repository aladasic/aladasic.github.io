abstract: "This Article examines the economic implications of the evolving payments system through four lenses: consumers, businesses, the financial sector, and the macroeconomy. It presents original findings from Central Bank of Ireland surveys on households and businesses, including evidence of a clear generational divide in payment behaviour and the importance consumers place on security, reliability, fraud protection and trust, alongside new evidence estimating the cost of retail payments in Ireland at approximately €1 billion annually, with small businesses facing disproportionately higher transaction costs. The Article also considers how faster, cheaper and more integrated payment systems can support trade, financial inclusion, innovation and business dynamism, while examining the implications of new forms of money and payment services for bank funding, monetary policy transmission, resilience and the future architecture of the monetary system."
